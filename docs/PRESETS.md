# Where the presets come from

The bank is generated by `tools/gen_presets.py` and compiled into the
plugin by `build.rs`. Each Classics preset's comment says which grade it is.

## Factory banks

| Bank | Patches | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Juno-106 A/B | 128 | Roland's factory SysEx, as decoded in [Ultramaster KR-106](https://github.com/kayrockscreenprinting/ultramaster_kr106) (GPL-3.0) |
| Juno-60 A/B | 111 | A Juno-60 factory tape dump, same source |

**Juno-106** values are stored as they were: this engine is the 106's.

**Juno-60** patches cannot be copied slider for slider, because the same
slider position means something else on each machine (at 0.5 the cutoff is
about 1 kHz on a 60 and about 750 Hz on a 106). `convert_j60` moves each
control to the 106 position that gives the same physical value:

| Control | Matched in |
|---|---|
| VCF freq | Hz |
| LFO rate | Hz |
| DCO LFO, VCF LFO | semitones |
| VCF env | octaves (106: 14 at full travel) |
| Attack, decay, release | ms |
| Sub, noise | level |
| HPF | 60 flat and 122 Hz → Flat; the other two by position |

Resonance, key follow, sustain, VCA level and PWM width are copied. So are
LFO delay (no 60 curve is available) and the switches. A Juno-60 mode with no
106 counterpart stays available: PWM from the envelope, and Chorus I+II.

Limits of that conversion:

- KR-106 marks its Juno-60 envelope, sub and DCO LFO curves as Juno-6
  measurements standing in for the 60, so those conversions are close, not
  exact. Its DCO range for 60 patches is partly inferred too: the 60's tape
  format does not store the range switch.
- The engine stays a 106: firmware envelopes, not the 60's analog ones. These
  are the 60's patches played on a 106.
- **B34 Drawbar Organ is missing.** All its slider bytes are zero in the tape
  dump, so the patch is lost. It was left out rather than invented.

Some factory patches carry a remark in the browser, from
[Synthmania](https://www.synthmania.com/juno-106.htm). Those are comparisons
("reminds of"), not claims that a record used the patch.

## Classics

- **Recipe**: settings published by the cited source. Most are from Reverb
  Machine's walk-throughs, which program the sounds on TAL U-NO-LX: Tame
  Impala ([1](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/tame-impala-synth-sounds/),
  [2](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/tame-impala-synths-pt-2/),
  [3](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/tame-impala-synths-pt-3/),
  [4](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/tame-impala-cause-im-a-man-synth-sounds/),
  [5](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/tame-impala-love-paranoia-synth-sounds/)),
  [Wham!](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/how-wham-created-last-christmas/),
  [Mac DeMarco](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/mac-demarco-synth-sounds/)
  ([One More Love Song](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/how-mac-demarco-created-one-more-love-song/)),
  [Porches](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/porches-synth-sounds/),
  [BADBADNOTGOOD](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/badbadnotgood-synth-sounds/),
  [Neon Indian](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/neon-indian-synth-sounds/),
  [Daft Punk](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/how-daft-punk-created-short-circuit/)
  and [Blood Orange](https://reverbmachine.com/blog/blood-orange-synth-sounds/).
- **Reconstruction**: no settings were published; the values are our estimate.
  These are *Time After Time*, *Take On Me*, *Can You Feel It* and *Girls Just
  Want to Have Fun*. The credits come from Roland's articles on
  [Rob Hyman](https://articles.roland.com/rob-hyman-the-hooters-time-after-time-and-the-juno-60/)
  and the [Juno-60](https://articles.roland.com/juno-60-the-whole-story/).

What to expect from a recipe:

- Nothing here was compared by ear with the records. The patches were checked
  for level and sanity by automated tests only.
- The articles keep many values in screenshots, which were not read. Whatever
  the text leaves out is a plain musical value, and the comment says
  "estimated" where that matters.
- The faders are TAL's 0-10, which do not map exactly to a 106. The HPF is a
  fader on TAL and a four-way switch here, so it snaps to the nearest position.
  A few filter or level values were raised where the literal setting came out
  nearly silent on this engine (*Yes I'm Changing Pad*, *Trap Bass*,
  *Dagenham Dream*).
- Effects after the synth (reverb, delay, amp sims, phasers) are not part of a
  preset.
- Not all of these records used a Juno. *Chamber of Reflection* was a JX-3P,
  and *One More Love Song* is unknown; the recipes show how to get there on a
  Juno.

*Time After Time*: Rob Hyman says "Number 61 on the JUNO" and calls it a pad,
without naming a bank. A61 on a factory Juno-60 is Synth Organ, which is what
the preset uses. It may not be the patch he meant.

## Sound Design

Programming recipes that are not tied to a record:
[Roland, Creating JUNO Basses](https://articles.roland.com/sound-design-creating-juno-basses/)
and Attack Magazine
([house bass](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/make-a-house-bass-with-the-juno-60/),
[Overmono pads](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/tutorials/overmono-style-pads-with-roland-cloud-juno-106/)).
Both program a plugin with two envelopes and built-in effects. On this engine
one envelope stands in for the two, and the effects are left to you.

## Regenerating

```sh
cargo run --release --example curves > tools/juno106_curves.csv
python3 tools/gen_presets.py <ultramaster_kr106>/Source/KR106_Presets_JUCE.h
```

`build.rs` needs a `bank/` of numeric-id presets to compile, so to rebuild the
curve table from nothing, empty `bank/` first.
